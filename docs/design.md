# Still Time: design notes

Entry for [Slapjam AI #2](https://itch.io/jam/slapjam-ai-2): theme **Speed**, a mobile portrait browser game, judged on theme, fun and visuals equally.

## The game

Top-down rooms full of enemies. **Time stops while you are still and runs while you move**: you read the bullets, arrows and knives coming at you, then choose when and where to move. One hit kills you (the room restarts), and one cut kills an enemy. Each room has a goal: down every enemy, or reach its exit. Doors split bigger rooms into chambers.

Every new piece must be readable **from a paused frame**: the player spends most of the game looking at frozen time, so what something does has to show on it (pips on a door, a crown, a key over a head), never hidden state. New things are taught one per room, alone first.

Speed is the choice the player makes: every move speeds the whole world up with you.

The rule is taught by its consequence, never by a caption alone. Playtests (October 2026) showed that three guided rooms with no threat in them (a stick drill, a clockwork corridor, a door on a timer) taught nothing: while moving, players watch the thumb and the target, never the surroundings, and nothing ever made stillness matter. They never noticed the analog speed either, and could not: the finger covers the stick, and a paused frame cannot show a half pull. So the game opens on a cutscene in which a bullet stops when you stop, the first fight rooms are unwinnable by charging (`test/teaching.test.ts` keeps them so), the hit screen names the rule, the world freezes exactly (not nearly), and the analog speed is a mastery detail, not a lesson.

## Rules (each has a headless test in `test/fight.test.ts`)

- **Touch: floating thumb-stick.** Press anywhere and drag: the stick appears where you pressed (a ring and a knob), still within 10 px, top speed (5 m/s) at 110 px; lift and you stop. It is never taught as a stick: the first room's bullet teaches "drag, and it moves; let go, and it stops", which is all a player needs (a drill of the stick was tried and dropped: it taught dragging, which nobody failed at). (Follow-the-finger and a trackpad drag were tried and dropped: a far press ran at full speed for free, and players spammed taps.) The freeze ring and sound need 0.4 s of running first. Keys: arrows or WASD.
- **World speed** follows how fast you actually walk: **0 when still** (a true freeze: the world clock stops, snapped exactly once within a hair of it), real time at top speed, eased quickly. (At ×0.04 a bullet 4 m away still took a motionless player in ten seconds, and taught that "still" is not safe.) Pushing into a wall (or a shut door, or an enemy) is standing still: you do not move, so neither does time. You move in real time and the world is what slows down, so slow motion helps you *read* a threat, not outrun it. The analog speed in between is real but never taught.
- **The hit screen names the rule.** A hit remembers what took you (a shot, a swing, a hazard, a blast, a stalker), and the screen says what the rule was ("you were moving. so was it. / stand still: it stops. then step aside"); the shot that took you hangs where it struck, ringed in gold. It is the most-read screen in the game.
- **Your cut:** any enemy within 1.15 m dies, except a guarded front (a shield-bearer's always, a clockwork's while it attacks).
- **Shooters** (gunner: bullets; archer: fast arrows; thrower: a fan of three knives; sniper, below) fire only with line of sight within 13 m, 0.25 s after you step out of cover if already reloaded. They hold fire and sidestep when an ally is in any line of their shot, creep toward where they last saw you, and stay within 3.5 m of their post.
- **Spearmen** (the `brute` kind) stand until they see you, then chase faster than you (5.5 m/s, a 180° turn in about 0.3 s). Within 2.9 m they hold their ground and wind up for 0.27 s, tracking you, then committed for the last 0.22 s; the thrust lands the step it starts, 2.7 m along a narrow ±17° line, then they recover for 0.8 s. They are never guarded: your cut always downs one, but the spear outreaches it, and a head-on rush covers only about 1.35 m of the wind-up (time runs only as you move, about 5 m per world second at full push), so the thrust lands first. Stop as it winds up, step off the line (or back), then cut in while it recovers. (It was a swordsman whose front was guarded while it attacked: walking up and cutting it from the front did nothing, which broke the one rule the game teaches, close = cut. The spear shows why you can't walk in: its reach.)
- **The melee family** shares the spearman's cycle (chase, wind-up tracking then committed, strike, recover) with its own numbers in `MELEE`, and a guard of `none`, `attack` or `always`. A guarded front stops your cut **and shots**.
- **Shield-bearer:** slow (2.6 m/s) and slow to turn (1.5 rad/s), its shield always up (±72°): a steel plate in front. Circling it within 3 m at full speed outpaces its turn; its back is open. Shots on the shield throw a spark.
- **Sniper:** sees 24 m (others 13), holds its post. While it sees you a dashed laser tracks you and charges (1.5 world s); hiding starts it over. For the last 0.4 s the laser **locks** (solid, blinking) and stops tracking: step off the line. Then one fast slug (30 m/s) down the locked line. It starts uncharged.
- **Breakable cover:** wood is what breaks. A **crate** splinters after 3 shots (cracks show as it wears), and hides you from no one: a shooter sees you through it and fires into it, so it is cover that lasts three shots, not a hiding place. A **cracked wall** (dark fissures with an orange glint) shrugs shots off but gives to a **brute's swing** (its blade bites 0.4 m past its reach) or a **blast**. A retry mends everything.
- **Grenadier:** lobs a bomb at where you stand, over its allies (it does not care who the blast takes). The bomb flies in world time, lands lit, and its fuse (1.3 world s) burns as a red sweep round a ring on the floor: the blast's reach (1.9 m). The blast takes everyone in that ring with nothing solid in between (you, any enemy, the grenadier), and breaks any cracked wall or crate it reaches.
- **Clockwork:** a slow brass brute (1.6 m/s) that keeps **real time**: it walks, winds up and swings at its own pace however still you stand. Its front is guarded while it attacks; the brute's dodge beats it.
- **Stalker:** creeps straight at you in real time, as fast as you are **still** (full 2.4 m/s when you stand frozen, none at top speed). While you are at least half still it strikes anyone within 1.35 m, just past your cut; its ring on the floor turns red. Hesitate near it and it takes you; rush it at top speed and it stays frozen for your cut.
- **Conveyor belts** carry you along them at 2.6 m/s in real time, push or no push: ride one still and the world stays frozen. They carry only you.
- **Friendly fire:** any shot kills any enemy it touches (a guarded front blocks it: a shield always, a clockwork while it attacks).
- **Score:** each attempt has a clock in **world** seconds, so it only runs while you move. A clear earns up to three stars: one for the clear, one for no hit in the room since you came into it (retries count), one for beating the room's par. The run keeps each room's best; the last screen totals them.
- **Goals:** `clear` (down every enemy), `exit` (stand on the glowing pad; enemies left standing don't matter), or `survive` (last N world seconds, which only pass while you move; the HUD's clock counts toward it).
- **Plates:** a door can be locked by pressure plates (teal; its door shows a square per plate, lit as each goes down). A plate presses as you step onto it and stays down. Numbered plates must go in order: a wrong one pops them all back up with a red flash.
- **Hazards** run on the attempt's world clock, so they freeze with you. A **blade** bar spins round its hub (its sweep dotted on the floor); a **laser gate** between two posts is off (faint), warns (blinking dashes, 0.45 world s), then on (solid red). Either takes anyone it touches, enemies too.
- **Doors** are walls until their lock is met, then open for the rest of the attempt (a retry shuts them). Locks: a **chamber** cleared (steel door, one red pip per enemy it still waits on), the chamber's **captain** down (gold door with a crown), or its **key** brought to it (a door in the key's colour; it opens when you reach it holding the key, and keeps the key). A **time** door (amber, with a bar filling) opens once the room's world clock has run its seconds: time runs only while you walk, so you open it by moving (pushing against it does nothing). Closed doors block movement, sight and shots for everyone.
- **Captain:** any enemy can wear a crown. Downing it opens its chamber's captain door; it fights like its kind.
- **Runner (thief):** harmless, carries a key over its head. While it sees you it runs (3.6 m/s, slower than you) toward whichever of 16 directions lands furthest from you; out of sight it stands still, so you sneak up behind cover. Wherever it falls (your cut, or a stray shot) it drops its key on the floor; walk over it to pick it up.
- **Solid bodies:** enemies never overlap each other or you. Brutes keep room from allies, steer round one in their path, and walk at 100% / 82% / 92% pace by spawn order, so they don't arrive in step.

## Look

Over your head, where the eyes are, a small pause sign while the world waits and a small play sign as soon as it runs at all; enemies on the move leave red afterimages while time runs, as you leave teal ones. The title plays the Prologue behind itself by the same director, without its words (an attract loop), so players see the rule before they press anything.

An upright 2D room with a slight Pokémon-style camera lean: mostly tops, short fronts (floor depth ×0.86, heights at half their size). Decals on the floor (shot paths, swing wedges, guard rings), crates and walls as boxes, people as cylinders with shadows, and your outline always on top. The floor turns cold blue when time is frozen and warm when it runs. The camera follows you with a small lead and stays inside the room.

The iso and steeper tilts were tried and rejected ("too isometric").

## Progress

Rooms open one by one: the first always, each next once the one before is cleared (the simulation refuses a jump to a locked room). The game opens on a title (continue at the first room not cleared, or pick a room); the room select shows every room with its best stars and world time, locked rooms dimmed. A menu pauses the game.

Progress is saved on the device, in the browser's local storage (itch.io has no save API for HTML games): each room's best, by room name, so a build that adds or reorders rooms keeps it. itch.io serves every HTML game from one origin, so every key starts with `still-time/`. The format is versioned; a save that cannot be read is kept aside (`still-time/progress.unreadable`) and the game starts fresh; when the storage cannot be read at all, nothing is written over it. Saves are per browser and device (no sync), and a browser may clear them.

## Rooms

Each room adds one thing, with few enemies, and the rooms grow from there:

1. Prologue: the rule, as a cutscene (a room with `scene`: the page plays it with your hands off, the HUD hidden, its words in a letterbox; each beat with words holds, the world frozen, until you tap, as long as you like, and "tap to continue" shows at once (minimum holds of up to 3 s were tried: players who had read the line were made to wait); "skip" in the top bar plays the rest at once). A director (`src/host/intro.ts`) drives it by reading the view each step, so a headless test plays it identically. You appear (the clear's vanish, backwards) and take a few steps up the corridor; as you walk (the world runs with you), a gunner walks in from out of view to its post about 9.5 m off (a spawn's `from`: a shooter walks in at 12 m per world second and neither looks nor fires until it is there), and a red "!" pops over it: it has seen you. Then, as you walk at it, it fires. The scene's camera is its own: it looks up the corridor ahead of you while the gunner enters, then holds a slow two-shot of you and it, and never centres on you as play's camera does; the control cues (your cut's ring, the pause sign) are hidden, the bars close in as it starts, and a softer, desaturated grade sits under them. (The words sit centred in a taller bottom bar; "skip" is centred in the top one.) You walk on at its bullet, ringed in gold, and ease to a stop just short of it as the world stops with you: "when you stop, / time stops." A slow step aside and it crawls past: "move slowly, / and it crawls." On toward the gunner, it fires again, and you stop short of that one too: "it waits / as long as you do." Close to the gunner, its body ringed: "you only need to / get close, to cut!"; you walk in and cut it; "now it's / your turn." (A first room you played, opening on a frozen bullet with captions, was tried: players slid aside without noticing the far, small bullet, and the big captions took their eyes. Lanes of bolts in the prologue were tried and dropped: their stop came and went before it was read.) 2. Crossing: the first room in your hands, and where the drag is taught. A hand shows the gesture low on the screen (it presses, a ring appears, it slides) under "press anywhere / and hold", then "now slide / your finger, any way"; any walk at all counts. Then "now let go / watch the bolts" until you have stood still a moment ("they stopped / with you"). Two streams of bolts cross the hall (one bolt on each lane at a time, the lanes half a beat apart), from launchers on the walls whose lanes start at their barrels' tips (`MUZZLE`, 0.9 m out; the page hides a bolt still inside a barrel): "they hang. look. / cross between them". Nothing in it moves on its own. 3. Time Door: the clock, along a corridor (guide: `src/host/guide.ts`), now with one time door of 3 seconds, a few steps from the start (three doors were too many; a walk runs the clock as fast as it goes, so a short door further off would be open before you reached it), "it takes time to open" in small letters over its timer; the clockwork stretch comes after it. First a stretch of wall lanterns swinging and wheels turning on the world clock (a room's `decor`, drawn by the page): "nothing moves / until you do", then "everything moves / with you" as you walk. Then three time doors in a row, each a few world seconds after the last; their portcullises rise over their last 3 seconds (the bars lifting into the beam) and a timer on the next shut one counts down only while you walk (pushing against the door does not count): "doors open with time", "it waits for you" while you stand at one. 4. First Cut: the cut, with gunners that never fire (`quiet` enemies). One in the first chamber, marked with gold brackets, a gold ring on the floor at touching range and chevrons: "get close / walk into the ring"; cut it and the door to the second chamber opens on two more, "get close to hit / 2 more inside". Each one cut pops "CUT!" over it; "got it! / close = cut" stays through the clear. 5. First Shot: one gunner that fires, in the open, two crates: the prologue's stop, step aside, walk in, now in your hands. 6. Courtyard: two gunners, sight and cover. 7. Duel: one spearman. 8. Two Spears: two spearmen. 9. Splinters: two gunners, and crates that splinter. 10. Switchyard: a plate opens the gate, one gunner. 11. Gatehouse: the first door (clear this side to open it), two gunners this side, one past it. 12. Friendly Fire: a thrower at the back of a line of two gunners. 13. Breach: the way out is a cracked wall; lure the spearman's thrust into it. 14. Throne: the captain, and the first exit. 15. Thief: the runner and its key. 16. Watchtower: one sniper down a long room of cover. 17. Shieldwall: a shield-bearer guarding a gunner. 18. Mill: two blades across a corridor, no enemies: a timing room. 19. Powder Room: a grenadier (and a gunner its blast can take). 20. Combination: numbered plates, a spearman and a gunner. 21. Gauntlet: two laser gates and a gunner. 22. Market: two chambers, and the first archers (four enemies). 23. Clockworks: one clockwork. 24. Stalker: one stalker. 25. Conveyor: a belt up the middle between two gunners. 26. Warehouse: a thief among shelves, archers down the aisles (five). 27. Hold Out: survive 9 s with a blade, a gate and three shooters. 28. Fortress: yard gate, keep key, and a crowned spearman at the captain's door.

The order (a playtest asked for it): after the four teaching rooms, gunners first (the tutorials' enemy), then the spearman; one new idea a room, alone before it is combined; at most two enemies until about room 10 and three until about room 21; the big rooms (Warehouse, Hold Out, Fortress) at the end.

The rule must stay needed: `test/teaching.test.ts` plays a "charger" (full push at the nearest enemy, never a stop) through First Shot, Courtyard, Duel, Two Spears, Friendly Fire and Gatehouse, twenty times each, and fails if it ever wins one. A room a charger can win has stopped teaching.

Pars are first guesses (a scripted dodge clears the Duel in 2.0 world s against a par of 4); tune them from play.

## How it was found (prototype rounds, October 2026)

1. Still-time rooms with three control variants. The player picked hold-and-drag ("Glide") over swipe-to-dash ("Flick"). It felt too fast, so it was asked to be analog.
2. Analog thumb, lower top speed, time following the push.
3. Made frenetic: shot speeds doubled, reloads halved. Open rooms bigger than the screen, a following camera, cover, line of sight.
4. The brute: a melee enemy you dodge and flank.
5. Brute made swordsman-fast. Shooters reposition and keep allies out of their fire. Found headless: the commit window is what makes a dodge possible.
6. Solid enemies, steering round allies, varied paces.
7–8. Projected view: iso rejected, the Pokémon-style low tilt kept.
9. Brute open from the front outside its attack. You can't walk through enemies. Camera edge fix.

## Roadmap

Ordered so each batch builds on the last, extensions before exceptions:

1. **Doors (done):** score and stars, doors and chambers, the captain, the runner and its key.
2. **Tougher enemies (done):** the shield-bearer (a front that also blocks shots), the sniper (a laser that charges in sight, locks, then fires). The knight (three hits with a daze) was built and cut: one cut kills, always.
3. **Things that break (done):** cracked walls a brute's swing breaks, the grenadier (a fuse ring; the blast kills allies and breaks walls), crates that shots wear down. The splitter was dropped: one cut kills, always.
4. **Puzzles and pressure (done):** plates (one, then numbered), survive rooms, timed hazards (blades, laser gates). Alarms and chain bonuses are not built: both hide state a frozen frame can't show.
5. **Rule-breakers (done):** the clockwork enemy (real time), the stalker (moves only while you are still), conveyor belts.

Still open: sound and music; brutes coordinating as a pack; alarms and chain bonuses (if a frozen frame can show them). Best stars are kept across sessions (see Progress).
