# Prototype: still time

Slapjam AI #2 (theme: Speed), mobile portrait browser game.

## Question

When time barely moves while you stand still and runs at full speed while you move, does picking *when* and *where* to move through an open room (camera follows you, walls and crates to hide behind) of incoming bullets, arrows and knives feel tense and smart? You clear a room by getting close enough to cut every enemy down (one hit kills, either way); a hit restarts the room.

Movement is analog: how far the thumb is from where it pressed sets your speed (top speed at 72 px), and time runs as fast as you move (Glide, chosen in round 5; the other control variants are gone).

Views (V or the bottom button, page-only): **iso** (room turned 45°, 2:1) and **tilt** (upright 3/4 view).

## Rounds

- Round 1: first build, 5 rooms, three enemy kinds (gunner, archer, thrower), enemy shots also kill enemies. Verdict: "Flick (the first one where I keep pressing) is the one" (the hold-and-drag Glide). "A little hard, too fast when I move." Wants speed controlled by how far the thumb is from the press point, and a slightly lower top speed.
- Round 2: analog stick (dead zone 8 px, full at 72 px, curve 1.4), top speed 6.5 → 5 m/s, the player moves in real time; Flick removed; new default makes time follow your speed, Glide·snap keeps the round-1 time rule with analog movement. Verdict: "This works." Wants it frenetic: faster bullets (speed and reload). Wants open rooms bigger than the screen, camera following the player, places to move and hide.
- Round 3: shot speeds doubled (bullet 9, arrow 18, knife 12 m/s), reloads about halved (0.85 / 1.3 / 1.1 s); rooms 14×24 up to 22×36 m with walls, shelves and crates; camera eased on the player with a little lead; enemies shoot only with line of sight within 13 m, firing 0.25 s after you step out if reloaded; off-screen enemy markers at the edge. Verdict: "This works." Next: enemies that move. A melee enemy that closes in with a weapon facing one way; when you are about to reach it, it stops and winds up the hit; you dodge the hit and swing round the sides or behind to cut it.
- Round 4: the brute. Idle until it sees you, then chases (2.8 m/s, turns 2.6 rad/s world). At 2.2 m it locks its facing and winds up (0.5 s), swings a 2 m wedge of ±57° (0.12 s), then recovers (0.9 s). Its front (±69°) is guarded: you cut it only from the side or behind. Enemy shots still kill it. New first rooms: Duel (one brute) and Two Blades; brutes added to Market, Warehouse, Fortress. Verdict: wanted it "way faster": entering its range and escaping should not be easy; it should turn 180° much faster, like a normal swordsman that just needs to get close. Wants shots to hit it too; shooters should not fire at random so much, should move a little to reposition, and should not shoot straight into an ally (move instead).
- Round 5: brute speed 2.8 → 5.5 m/s (faster than you), turn 2.6 → 10 rad/s; the wind-up (0.5 s) tracks you and steps in to 1.6 m, then commits for the last 0.35 s (dashed wedge → solid filling wedge), swing 0.1 s, recover 0.8 s. Found headless: with time following your speed, slow-mo gives no extra dodge distance, so the commit window is what makes a dodge possible (step back out of reach, then flank during recovery). Shooters: hold fire and sidestep when an ally is in any line of their shot (thrower: each knife of the fan), creep toward where they last saw you when you hide, leashed 3.5 m from their post. Enemy shots already killed brutes. Verdict: two of the same enemy should not close in at the same pace, they end up overlapping. Wants enemies to have hitboxes so they steer round an ally instead of walking on top of each other.
- Round 6: enemies are solid (overlaps pushed apart each step; shooters give way less than brutes). A chasing brute keeps 0.7 m of room from other enemies and steers to the open side of any ally on its path within 2.5 m. Brutes walk at paces 100% / 82% / 92% in spawn order. Headless, three brutes in a column: closest pair 1.42 m apart (touching is 0.96), the second fanned out sideways behind the first. Verdict: "This works." Next: a more isometric view, still 2D; drop the control variants, Glide works.
- Round 7: control variants removed (time follows your push, always). New projected renderer: floor and decals (shot paths, swing wedges, guard rings, reach) on the ground; crates and walls as 1.2 m boxes and back walls; people as cylinders with shadows; painted back to front with a box-vs-point rule; your outline always drawn on top so walls never hide you. Your thumb's screen direction is turned into the world direction that looks the same. The camera keeps the room's on-screen box filling the view. Two views to compare: iso and tilt. Verdict: _not played yet_
